## The problem

An authority asks where a number came from. Somebody spends three days rebuilding the answer out of an inbox, a shared drive, an accounting system, and the memory of a colleague who has since left.

That is an evidence problem rather than a filing problem, and it repeats every cycle. The records exist across the systems you already run. They are not gathered, not linked to the claims they support, and not held in a form that survives the question "prove it".

Smaller organisations feel it hardest, because obligations arrive at the same rate as they do for a large company while the compliance department does not exist.

## What Citadel is

A governed store for the records your organisation already produces. Material flows in from the systems you run. Each item is checked on the way in, recorded with where it came from, held under access rules you control, and logged when it is read or changed.

When a report needs backing, the evidence is gathered and linked already. You produce the report from records, and when someone asks where a figure came from, the answer is a document and a timestamp.

The same store answers questions directly. Your staff ask it, and so do AI assistants, under the same identity and access rules as the person operating them.

## It fits what you already run

Nothing has to be migrated first and nothing has to be reorganised to suit us. Citadel reads from the systems you already operate, and keeps its own indexed copy of what it takes in. Your document store stays where it is and stays authoritative.

Because that copy sits inside your instance, a report filed today can still be defended after the source document has been edited, moved, or deleted. The trail does not depend on anyone else's retention policy. It does depend on yours, which is the next section.

## Three kinds of trace, and where each one stands

**Evidence trace.** *Partly built.* A figure resolves to the document behind it and to the copy Citadel holds. Resolving every retrieved item to an attested capture-time record, so a third party can check it without trusting our node, is designed and not built.

**Process trace.** *Partly built.* Who did what, when, and with what outcome. Agent tool calls and the main API surfaces are logged today. Extending that to every route, with durable retention rather than a live view that resets, is scoped work.

**Agent trace.** *Designed, not built.* Which sources an assistant retrieved, and which of them shaped what it produced. As organisations put assistants closer to regulated data, "which document made the model say that" stops being an academic question. Very little on the market answers it.

## Where your data sits, and what leaves

Retrieval, access control, embeddings, and the audit trail run inside your instance. Embeddings are computed locally by default.

Five features call an external model service today: building the knowledge graph when material is ingested, optional content enrichment, promotion classification, the organisation digest, and the self-improvement pass. Enrichment ships off by default and the digest has its own switch. Promotion classification and the self-improvement pass do not have one yet. Removing the model API key stops all five and costs you graph construction.

European or on-premise inference for every step that calls a model, behind a single deployment-wide switch, is specified and not yet shipped. Until it is, a deployment that must make no external model call runs without a model API key.

That paragraph is the one a competitor will quote. It is here because you would find it anyway, and because the alternative is discovering it at a technical review.

## Data protection, retention, and getting out

**Retention.** The product's benefit, that a retained copy survives deletion at the source, is an obligation as much as a feature. Retention is configured per obligation with a defined maximum, erasure either propagates to retained copies or records a legal-hold justification, and the lawful basis for retention is the reporting obligation itself. `[[State which of this is built and which is roadmap before sending. Do not guess.]]`

**For the hosted option.** A data processing agreement, a sub-processor list, a stated hosting region, and an encryption statement are required before a first hosted customer, and `[[confirm which of these exist today]]`. The external model service named above is a sub-processor and has to appear on that list. We hold no ISO 27001 or SOC 2 certification.

**Exit.** Data lives in standard PostgreSQL and you can take a dump whenever you like. The software is Apache-2.0, so you can keep running it with or without us. On termination of a hosted instance you get a final export and written confirmation of deletion.

## What works today

Records flow in automatically from a GitHub organisation, from repository content, from an issue tracker, from an Obsidian vault, and from approved folders on a workstation. Webhooks and configurable connectors for other systems are how a partner adds their own sources without waiting on us.

Content entering the knowledge vault passes a secret scanner before it is indexed. It is one gate, shared by the HTTP ingest endpoint, the contribute endpoint, the Obsidian sync, the CLI autosync, and the writer tools an AI assistant uses.

Access is bound per person and per organisation. Every search hit returns with its dataset, its rank, a provenance envelope, and a SHA-256 digest of the result as served, which detects corruption and accidental change in transit.

People reach it through a command-line tool, a web interface, or an HTTP API. AI assistants connect over MCP under role-scoped access.

## The roadmap, and how we gate it

Four stages. Each one exits on something measurable rather than on a date, and we would rather tell you the sequence than invent a calendar we cannot hold. Dates come with a pilot scope, against your systems.

**Stage one, stabilisation.** Upgrade the knowledge engine to the current upstream release. Move the graph store onto PostgreSQL, which removes an embedded single-writer database and the lock contention that comes with it. Make every ingested document retrievable, since indexing a document and being able to find it are not the same thing and we measure both. *Exit: every open issue closed and a full run start to finish on both the assistant and command-line surfaces.*

**Stage two, validation.** Benchmark and stress test against our own organisation's live vault before any of it is offered to anyone else. *Exit: published numbers for search latency, ingest throughput, and what it costs to operate, generated by a named harness rather than written by hand.*

**Stage three, the evidence layer.** Attested capture-time fingerprints held with each record. Per-item provenance on everything rather than on search hits alone. Contradiction and staleness detection. Export packages carrying source links, timestamps, and fingerprints. *Exit: a figure in a report resolves, end to end, to a record a third party can verify without trusting our node.*

**Stage four, the white-label product.** The connector and webhook contract that lets a partner add their own systems without waiting on us. European or on-premise inference for every model call, behind one switch. Packaging, theming, and per-deployment configuration. *Exit: a partner deploys, brands, and connects their own source without us writing code for them.*

Stages one and two are underway and self-funded. Stage three is what a project accelerates, and it is the stage that turns "we keep good records" into "an auditor can check this without taking our word for it".

## What this is not

Not a governance, risk, and compliance platform for tracking controls and policies. Not a reporting suite that produces the filing itself. Not a document management system, since your existing one stays authoritative. Not enterprise search, because search without provenance and an audit trail does not survive being asked to prove anything.

Citadel is the evidence layer underneath whichever of those you already own.

## The boundary we will not blur

Citadel assembles and proves the evidence behind a report. Producing a submission in the exact format a particular authority accepts is connector work, scoped per pilot against that authority's actual specification.

Any vendor claiming out-of-the-box submission to any EU authority is describing something that does not exist. Formats differ per obligation, per member state, and per year. What generalises is the layer underneath, and that is what makes the format work small rather than large.
